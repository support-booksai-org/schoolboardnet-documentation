# Accessibility First

## Quick Summary

Accessibility means people can use information even if they see, hear, move, or read differently.

schoolboard.net is designed to help districts publish board materials that more people can use.

## What Accessibility Means

Accessible content works for people who may use:

- A screen reader.
- A keyboard instead of a mouse.
- A phone or tablet.
- Large text.
- High contrast settings.
- Translation tools.
- Search tools.

Accessibility also helps people who are busy, tired, or using a small screen.

## Why Accessibility Matters for Board Materials

Board agendas and supporting documents are public records.

The public should be able to read and understand them.

Board members and staff should also be able to use them without unnecessary barriers.

## The Problem with PDF-Only Workflows

PDF files can be made accessible, but it takes care and skill.

Many PDFs are difficult to use because they may have:

- Missing headings.
- Poor reading order.
- Scanned images of text.
- Tables that screen readers cannot understand.
- Links that are not clear.
- Missing document titles.
- No useful structure.

When a district posts many PDFs, the accessibility work can become hard to manage.

## The schoolboard.net Approach

schoolboard.net encourages districts to place important agenda text directly into accessible HTML.

HTML is the normal language of the web.

When used correctly, it is easier for screen readers, search engines, and mobile devices.

## Accessibility Tip

Use clear headings.

Headings are like road signs. They help readers and screen readers understand where they are.

## Best Practice

Do not use a PDF just because the original document came from Word or Google Docs.

If the content can be placed cleanly into Expandable HTML, that is often a better choice.

## Common Mistake

Do not paste large blocks of messy Word formatting into the editor.

Clean text and proper headings create better accessible content.
